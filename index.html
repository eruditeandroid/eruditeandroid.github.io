John S. Nolan

I am a Visiting Assistant Professor in the Department of Mathematics at The Ohio State University. My research focuses on algebraic geometry and homological mirror symmetry, especially in the context of toric varieties and stacks.

I completed my PhD at UC Berkeley under the supervision of Constantin Teleman.

Current and Recent Projects

  1. A Functorial Classification of Toric Stacks. In this paper, I describe an approach to classifying toric stacks via stacky fans. Most previous approaches to this classification problem consider only those toric stacks which arise as quotient stacks of toric varieties. This is fine in the smooth case, but it fails to account for certain singular examples. We correct this by introducing a local theory (based on the notion of "toric categories") that encompasses all toric stacks. This project builds on some of the concepts developed in my PhD thesis but is logically independent (in particular, it does not require any infinity-categories!).
  2. Grouplike Symmetric Monoidal Categories and Quiver Tensor Products. My PhD thesis. This contains a rough sketch of an approach to "derived toric geometry" based on a theory of "grouplike symmetric monoidal infinity-categories." I hope to return to this someday and flesh the results out more.
  3. Geometric Construction of Quiver Tensor Products. With Daigo Ito. In this project, we studied a family of "extended convolution" tensor products on derived categories of coherent sheaves on toric varieties. These can be used to answer natural questions about tensor products in homological mirror symmetry. We also construct some previously unknown examples of monoidal structures on derived categories of projective spaces.
